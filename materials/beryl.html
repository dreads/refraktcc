Beryl

Be₃Al₂Si₆O₁₈ · Beryllium Aluminum Silicate

Beryl is a mineral family that includes some of the world's most prized gemstones. Pure beryl is colorless, but trace elements create a rainbow of gem varieties. The hexagonal crystal structure of beryl often produces the elongated, six-sided crystals that collectors prize.

With a hardness of 7.5-8 on the Mohs scale, beryl gems are durable enough for all types of jewelry. The name "beryl" comes from the Greek "beryllos," which was used to describe any green gemstone in ancient times.

Properties

Hardness
7.5 - 8 (Mohs scale)
Crystal System
Hexagonal
Refractive Index
1.565 - 1.602
Specific Gravity
2.63 - 2.92

Gem Varieties

  • Emerald — Green beryl colored by chromium and/or vanadium
  • Aquamarine — Blue to blue-green beryl colored by iron
  • Morganite — Pink to peach beryl colored by manganese
  • Heliodor — Yellow to golden beryl colored by iron
  • Goshenite — Colorless beryl
  • Red Beryl (Bixbite) — Extremely rare red variety from Utah
