Tourmaline

Complex Borosilicate

Tourmaline is actually a group of related minerals with the same crystal structure but varying chemical compositions. This chemical variability gives tourmaline the widest color range of any gemstone—it occurs in every color of the rainbow, often with multiple colors in a single crystal.

Tourmaline crystals are piezoelectric and pyroelectric, meaning they generate an electrical charge when heated or put under pressure. With a hardness of 7-7.5, tourmaline is suitable for all types of jewelry. Its strong pleochroism means colors can appear different when viewed from different angles.

Gem Varieties

  • Rubellite — Pink to red tourmaline
  • Indicolite — Blue tourmaline
  • Paraíba — Neon blue-green, copper-bearing (extremely rare)
  • Verdelite — Green tourmaline
  • Watermelon — Pink center with green rim
  • Chrome Tourmaline — Intense green colored by chromium
  • Schorl — Black tourmaline (most common variety)
