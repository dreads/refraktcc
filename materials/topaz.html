Topaz

Al₂SiO₄(F,OH)₂ · Aluminum Fluorosilicate

Topaz is a hard, brilliant gemstone prized for its clarity and range of colors. With a hardness of 8 on the Mohs scale, it's one of the hardest silicate minerals and takes an excellent polish. Pure topaz is colorless, but impurities create yellow, orange, pink, and blue varieties.

The most valuable topaz is Imperial Topaz from Brazil's Ouro Preto region, displaying rich orange to pink-orange colors. Most blue topaz on the market is white topaz that has been irradiated and heat-treated. Topaz has perfect basal cleavage, meaning it can split along one plane, so care must be taken during cutting and setting.

Gem Varieties

  • Imperial Topaz — Prized orange to pink-orange, naturally colored
  • Blue Topaz — Usually treated; comes in Sky, Swiss, and London blue
  • White Topaz — Colorless, often used as diamond alternative
  • Pink Topaz — Rare natural; often heat-treated Imperial
  • Sherry Topaz — Brownish-orange to yellow-brown
