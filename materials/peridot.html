Peridot

(Mg,Fe)₂SiO₄ · Magnesium Iron Silicate (Olivine)

Peridot is the gem-quality variety of the mineral olivine. Unlike most gemstones that get their color from impurities, peridot's distinctive yellow-green color comes from iron in its essential chemical structure—making it one of the few "idiochromatic" gems.

Peridot forms deep in the Earth's mantle and reaches the surface through volcanic activity. It has even been found in meteorites and on Mars. The ancient Egyptians called it the "gem of the sun" and mined it on the island of Zabargad in the Red Sea for over 3,500 years. Characteristic "lily pad" inclusions are common and help identify natural peridot.

Sources & Notes

  • Arizona (San Carlos) — Most of today's commercial peridot
  • Pakistan (Kashmir) — Fine, larger stones with intense color
  • Myanmar (Burma) — Historic source of quality material
  • China — Major producer of commercial-grade stones
  • Zabargad (Egypt) — Historic source, now largely exhausted
  • Pallasitic Peridot — Extraterrestrial gems from meteorites
