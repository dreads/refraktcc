Garnet

X₃Y₂(SiO₄)₃ · Silicate Mineral Group

Garnets are a group of silicate minerals that have been used as gemstones for thousands of years. While red garnets are most familiar, the group actually occurs in every color except blue. All garnets share the same crystal structure but have varying chemical compositions.

Garnets are relatively hard (6.5-7.5 on Mohs scale) and have no cleavage, making them durable for jewelry. Their high refractive index gives them excellent brilliance. The name "garnet" comes from the Latin "granatum" (pomegranate), referring to the red seeds of the fruit.

Properties

Hardness
6.5 - 7.5 (Mohs scale)
Crystal System
Isometric (Cubic)
Refractive Index
1.714 - 1.888
Specific Gravity
3.47 - 4.15

Gem Varieties

  • Almandine — Deep red to brownish-red, most common garnet
  • Pyrope — Blood red, often called "Bohemian garnet"
  • Spessartine — Orange to reddish-orange ("Mandarin garnet")
  • Grossular — Includes green tsavorite and orange hessonite
  • Tsavorite — Vivid green grossular, rivals emerald
  • Demantoid — Green andradite, known for "horsetail" inclusions
  • Rhodolite — Purple-red pyrope-almandine blend
  • Color-change Garnet — Shifts color under different lighting
