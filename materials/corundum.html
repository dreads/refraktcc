Corundum

Al₂O₃ · Aluminum Oxide

Corundum is the second hardest natural mineral after diamond, rating 9 on the Mohs scale. This exceptional hardness makes it ideal for fine jewelry that can withstand daily wear. Pure corundum is colorless, but trace elements create the stunning colors we associate with its gem varieties.

When corundum contains chromium, it displays the deep red color we know as ruby. All other colors of gem-quality corundum are called sapphire, though blue sapphires colored by iron and titanium are the most famous. Sapphires can also be pink, yellow, orange, green, or even color-changing.

Gem Varieties

  • Ruby — Red corundum colored by chromium
  • Sapphire — All other colors; blue from iron and titanium, pink from chromium, yellow from iron
  • Padparadscha — Rare pink-orange sapphire
  • Star Sapphire/Ruby — Displays asterism from rutile needle inclusions
