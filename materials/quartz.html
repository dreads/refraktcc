Quartz

SiO₂ · Silicon Dioxide

Quartz is the second most abundant mineral in Earth's crust and one of the most versatile gem materials. Its availability, durability, and range of colors make it popular with gem cutters and collectors alike. Quartz comes in two main forms: macrocrystalline (large crystals) and cryptocrystalline (microcrystalline aggregates like chalcedony and agate).

With a hardness of 7 on the Mohs scale, quartz is durable enough for everyday jewelry. Its trigonal crystal structure often produces the distinctive six-sided prismatic crystals that have fascinated humans for millennia.

Gem Varieties

  • Amethyst — Purple quartz colored by iron and irradiation
  • Citrine — Yellow to orange quartz; natural or heat-treated amethyst
  • Rose Quartz — Pink quartz, usually translucent
  • Smoky Quartz — Brown to gray quartz from natural irradiation
  • Rock Crystal — Colorless, transparent quartz
  • Ametrine — Bicolored purple and yellow quartz from Bolivia
  • Prasiolite — Green quartz, usually heat-treated amethyst
